# Where Vertical/SMB Software Incumbents Fail Their Users (2024–2026): Openings for a Focused New Entrant

> Method note for the report writer: ~38 web searches were run on 2026-10-24. Direct page fetching was blocked in this environment (WebFetch DNS errors; the egress proxy refused techcrunch.com, reddit.com, trustpilot.com, community.blackbaud.com and others), so **I could not open G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Reddit or app-store pages directly**. All "quotes" below came through search-tool excerpts of the cited pages. Treat them as likely verbatim but **check them against the live page before publishing**. Many pricing figures come from competitor blogs or aggregators (costbench, checkthat.ai, vibefam, projul, getonecrew), and these are flagged. I gathered no app-store (iOS/Google Play) review data, and no Reddit thread was retrieved directly.

## Q1. Which vertical SaaS products get consistent 1–3 star complaints, and what exactly do reviewers hate?

### Takeaway
The same five complaints repeat across verticals: (1) opaque, quote-only pricing with annual renewal hikes; (2) multi-year auto-renewing contracts with large early-termination fees; (3) new payment or processing fees pushed onto the SMB or its customers; (4) complexity and "too many clicks" in products built for bigger firms; (5) slow or chat-only support. The strongest, best-documented pain is in field service (ServiceTitan), boutique fitness (Mindbody), property management (AppFolio/Buildium fees), construction (Procore for small GCs), insurance agency management (Applied Epic/AMS360), therapy EHR (SimplePractice), nonprofit CRM (Blackbaud) and restaurant POS (Toast fees).

### Cited Findings

- I found no evidence of a price hike or widespread complaints. Jackrabbit Dance is rated 4.6/5 (748 reviews) from $49 — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.ca/compare/93349/131208/jackrabbit-dance/vs/dancebiz).

### Inferences
- The SMBs aren't complaining that the software doesn't work. They're complaining about the commercial relationship: hidden fees, renewal hikes, contracts that are hard to exit, and fees charged to their own customers. A new entrant can win on transparent monthly pricing, no lock-in and free data export, but that is easy for others to copy, so it can't be the only moat.
- "Too many clicks / built for bigger firms" shows up strongest where legacy desktop-era systems still dominate: insurance AMS, dental, vet, broker TMS and enterprise EHS.
- Mobile complaints were strongest in home care (the caregiver app plus EVV/GPS). I collected no app-store data, so mobile-specific pain elsewhere is under-evidenced.

### Gaps
- No direct app-store review data was retrieved, and no Reddit threads were read first-hand (network blocks).
- Most price figures for ServiceTitan, Procore, Mindbody and Aljex are third-party or competitor estimates. No vendor confirmed them.
- I found no reliable data on how many customers actually churned after any of these price changes.

## Q2. Which segments are being abandoned by incumbents (price hikes, moving upmarket, forced migrations, PE degradation, sunsets) in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented "abandonment" events of 2024–2026 are: forced product migrations (Applied TAM sunset; QuickBooks Desktop sales stopped for new US Plus/Payroll subscriptions in 2024); price restructurings that hit the smallest customers hardest (SimplePractice Starter +69% in March 2025; Fresha ended its free plan in early 2025; Planning Center's first increase in 18 years in Oct 2024; Blackbaud's new form fees; Toast's reversed 99¢ fee in July 2025); upmarket drift (ServiceTitan, at about $78K average annual billings per active customer); and consolidation (Mindbody/ClassPass/Booker's parent Playlist merged with EGYM in a $7.5B deal that closed March 2026). A regulatory forcing function relevant to an Israeli founder is Israel's e-invoicing allocation-number mandate, which dropped to NIS 5,000 in June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Applied TAM sunset (insurance agencies):** Applied is "sunsetting Applied TAM" because its architecture "will soon no longer receive support from Microsoft". It steers agencies to Applied Epic or EZLynx, and reports that "157 Applied TAM agencies migrated last year alone" — [Applied blog](https://int.appliedsystems.com/en-ca/blog/posts/why-were-sunsetting-applied-tam-and-whats-next-for-brokers/); [TAM migration hub](https://insight.useindio.com/en-us/tam-migration-resource-hub). One reviewer said they were "forced to by applied after they dropped support for TAM" — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/crm/applied-epic-profile/). An integrator: "the migration pain is rarely the new software — it is moving decades of policy, client, and accounting data cleanly" — [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/automate-insurance-agencies-migrate-off-applied-tam-to-epic-2026). I found no fixed end-of-life date.
- **QuickBooks Desktop:** Intuit stopped selling new subscriptions to Desktop Pro Plus, Premier Plus, Mac Plus and Enhanced Payroll in the US after July 31, 2024 (an Intuit community reply says Sept 30, 2024). Existing subscribers can keep renewing, but a ProAdvisor commentary notes Intuit can change this "without any notice" — [Insightful Accountant](https://insightfulaccountant.com/accounting-tech/general-ledger/is-july-31-2024-the-end-of-quickbooks-desktop); [Withum](https://withum.com/resources/the-impending-quickbooks-desktop-discontinuation-how-your-business-is-impacted). An earlier forced cutoff in 2023 was called "heavy-handed" by users — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/06/06/intuit_quickbooks_licensing/).
- **SimplePractice (therapists):** Starter rose from $29 to $49/mo in March 2025 — [Micro-SaaS Corner](https://mihais7.substack.com/p/simplepractice-just-raised-its-prices).
- **Fresha (salons):** The free-forever plan was removed in early 2025 — [Pabau](https://pabau.com/blog/fresha-pricing/).
- **Planning Center (churches):** First price increase in 18 years, effective for existing customers after Oct 9, 2024 — [Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/blog/2024/07/pricing-update).
- **Toast (restaurants):** The 99¢ online-order fee was introduced July 10, 2025 and reversed after backlash — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/toast-remove-99-cent-fee-after-widespread-backlash).
- **Blackbaud (nonprofits):** New online-form fees came with about 43 days' notice — [Blackbaud Community](https://community.blackbaud.com/discussion/comment/313910).
- **ServiceTitan upmarket drift:** Customers range from "family-owned contractors with a few employees to large franchises with... over 500 locations" — [SEC DRS/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1638826/000095012324003352/filename1.htm). The average active customer pays about $78K a year — [Meritech](https://meritech.substack.com/p/servicetitan-s-1-breakdown).
- **Mindbody consolidation (PE):** Vista took Mindbody private in 2019 — [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/mindbody-acquires-classpass-secures-500m-investment/). Mindbody acquired ClassPass in 2021 — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/mindbody-acquires-classpass-raises-500m/). It made repeated layoffs, including at ClassPass — [American Spa](https://www.americanspa.com/industry-news/layoffs-occur-mindbody-classpass). Playlist (parent of Mindbody, Booker and ClassPass) and EGYM agreed to merge at a $7.5B enterprise value with $785M of new equity (Vista, Temasek, L Catterton, Affinity Partners) and closed on March 31, 2026. The combined company has more than 40,000 Mindbody-powered businesses — [Playlist press](https://www.playlist.com/press/playlist-and-egym-announce-agreement-merge-and-785-million-new-equity-investments-bringing); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/classpass-mindbody-playlist-egym-merger-7-5-billion-deal/).
- **Acquisition-driven price jumps:** A Tripleseat reviewer said that after Tripleseat acquired their previous event software, "the price went up substantially" — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.be/software/111215/tripleseat).
- **PE / M&A backdrop:** SaaS deals hit an all-time high of 2,698 transactions in 2025, and PE made up about 57% of SaaS deals in Q1 2026 — [Baker Tilly](https://techma.bakertilly.es/en/reports/saas-sector-report-2026/). Vertical SaaS took 54% of SaaS M&A at a 5.8x multiple, a 41% premium over horizontal — [SaasRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/vertical-saas-ma-vc-report-2026). A low-rigor trade site describes the roll-up playbook as "raising prices 15-30%" with 20–40% headcount cuts. I found no primary evidence for this — [Algeria Tech News](https://algeriatech.news/saas-consolidation-pe-buyouts-2026/).
- **Israel e-invoicing mandate ("Israel Invoices" / allocation numbers):** Domestic B2B invoices above the threshold need a 9-digit allocation number from the Israel Tax Authority (ITA), or the buyer cannot deduct input VAT. The threshold was NIS 20,000 in 2025, NIS 10,000 from Jan 1, 2026, and NIS 5,000 from June 1, 2026 — [Comarch](https://www.comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/israel-accelerates-timeline-for-mandatory-allocation-numbers-in-invoicing/); [VATit](https://vatit.com/es/e-invoicing-guide/israel/); [ecosio](https://ecosio.com/en/compliance/israel/e-invoicing/).

### Inferences
- Forced migrations, such as the Applied TAM sunset or the QuickBooks Desktop phase-out, are the best moments to take customers: the customer has to switch anyway, so the incumbent's switching-cost moat disappears for a while.
- Consolidators chasing big strategic deals (Playlist/EGYM, focused on enterprise gym and wellness benefits) tend to under-invest in the smallest single-location customers. This is inference, not evidence: I found no 2025–26 data on Mindbody product quality.
- The Israeli allocation-number mandate makes US-built vertical tools (Jobber, Mindbody, etc.) structurally unfit for Israeli B2B SMBs unless they integrate with ITA. That is a localization wedge, but only for B2B-invoicing verticals. B2C is out of scope of the mandate — [ecosio](https://ecosio.com/en/compliance/israel/e-invoicing/).

### Gaps
- I found no confirmed Applied TAM end-of-life date and no number of remaining TAM agencies.
- No 2025–26 evidence on Mindbody product degradation after the merger. The only layoff coverage is undated or older.
- No systematic data on PE-driven price hikes in SMB vertical software. The 15–30% figure is unsourced.
- I didn't research which Israeli vertical tools already integrate with ITA allocation numbers. Morning/Green Invoice, iCount and Rivhit are known invoicing players, but I didn't verify them in this session.

## Q3. Recent examples of startups that won by unbundling or serving a hated incumbent's underserved segment, and how long it took

### Takeaway
The pattern holds, but it usually takes 5–10+ years and real capital. Owner.com (restaurant direct ordering, unbundling DoorDash and Toast online ordering) reached a $1B valuation in May 2025, about 5–7 years after founding. Mangomint (salon software aimed at 5–20-chair operators who find Mindbody dated) raised a $35M Series B in Sept 2024 with 100%+ ARR growth for several years. Jobber, Housecall Pro and FieldPulse have taken the sub-ServiceTitan SMB field-service segment, which is why that segment is now crowded.

### Cited Findings
- **Owner.com:** raised $120M Series C at a $1B valuation (May 2025), led by Meritech and Headline. It serves more than 10,000 restaurants and has "multiplied its annual recurring revenue every year since it was founded" — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/tech-supplier-ownercom-raises-120m-giving-it-1b-valuation); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-13/restaurant-tech-startup-owner-com-hits-1-billion-valuation). Sacra estimates ARR at about $34M at the end of 2024 and about $81M in 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/owner). The Series B in Jan 2024 was $33M at a $200M valuation — [WSGR](https://ecp.wsgr.com/news/wilson-sonsini-advises-ownercom-on-dollar33-million-series-b-financing). A reported Aug 2026 round of $240M at $2.3B is unverified (single source).
- **Mangomint:** a $35M Series B led by Altos Ventures (Sept 2024), with ARR and customer growth of "more than 100% for several years" (company claim) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/06/how-a-cold-email-to-a-vc-helped-salon-software-startup-mangomint-raise-35m). - **Field service challengers:** Jobber's latest round was $61M (March 2024), and its 100,000-user milestone was reported earlier — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/fieldpulse-vs-octopusapp); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2260400). ### Inferences
- From the cases found, a focused entrant typically needs about 2–4 years to reach meaningful ARR and 5–7 years to reach category-leader scale (Owner.com, Mangomint). Both raised substantial venture capital, so for a solo or small team "winning" more likely means a profitable niche ($1–5M ARR) than a unicorn.
- Successful wedges either attacked one painful workflow the incumbent did badly (Owner: commission-free direct ordering) or one size band the incumbent priced out (Mangomint: 5–20 chairs; Jobber/HCP: under 5 technicians).

### Gaps
- No verified founding dates or time-to-$1M-ARR for Mangomint, Jobber or FieldPulse.
- I didn't find solo or bootstrapped vertical-SaaS success cases with numbers for 2024–26. The YC vertical SaaS list wasn't retrieved.
- I found no Israeli-founded SMB vertical-SaaS success examples in this session.

## Q4. For each candidate category: market signals, price paid, switching costs, devil's-advocate argument, and verdict

### Takeaway
Most "obviously hated" categories (field service, salon, fitness, small-landlord property management, restaurant POS, therapy EHR, nonprofit CRM, church) now have several well-funded challengers, so a me-too entrant would be DROPPED. The openings that SURVIVE are narrower: (a) insurance agency management for small independent agencies, timed to the Applied TAM sunset; (b) Israeli-localized vertical operations software with built-in ITA allocation-number invoicing; (c) EHS/safety compliance for companies under 50 employees, by sub-vertical; (d) small freight brokerages priced out of Aljex/McLeod (low confidence); and (e) construction specialty-subcontractor workflows (narrow, low confidence).

### Cited Findings (category scorecard; all facts are sourced in Q1–Q3 unless a new link is given)

1. **Field service management (HVAC/plumbing, under 5 technicians)**
   - Price paid: ServiceTitan about $245–$500 per tech per month, average about $78K/yr per active customer; Jobber $169–$599/mo; Housecall Pro $59–$299/mo — [getonecrew](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/servicetitan-pricing); [Meritech](https://meritech.substack.com/p/servicetitan-s-1-breakdown); [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/field-service-management/housecall-pro/).
   - Switching cost: ServiceTitan exits run $2K to $29K+ in contract terms. SMB tools are low.
   - Devil's advocate: the under-5-tech segment already has Jobber ($61M, 2024), FieldPulse ($50M, 2025), Housecall Pro, Workiz and Zuper. ServiceTitan also sells a smaller product, ServiceTitan Pro.
   - **Verdict: DROPPED.** Crowded with funded challengers.

2. **Small-landlord property management (1–50 units)**
   - Price paid: AppFolio about $280/mo minimum (50 units); Buildium from $62/mo; TenantCloud from $15/mo; DoorLoop from $69/mo — [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/appfolio/alternatives).
   - Pain: fee creep (ACH fees, 2–3 hikes a year at Buildium).
   - Devil's advocate: Baselane ($34.4M, 2025), TurboTenant, RentRedi and TenantCloud are often free or cheap and monetize payments. Landlords' willingness to pay is low.
   - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

3. **Salon/spa booking**
   - Price paid: Fresha $14.95 per member/mo plus 20% on new marketplace clients.
   - Pain: Fresha's free plan ended in 2025; Mindbody lock-in.
   - Devil's advocate: Mangomint, Boulevard, GlossGenius, Vagaro and Square already compete hard, and booking is commoditized.
   - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

4. **Boutique fitness/wellness studio management**
   - Price paid: Mindbody $99–$699/mo per location plus processing. More than 40,000 Mindbody businesses.
   - Pain is strong (contracts, hikes, export fees), and the parent is distracted by the EGYM merger.
   - Devil's advocate: WellnessLiving, Punchpass, Arketa, Momence and others already court Mindbody switchers. Payments margin drives the economics and needs scale.
   - **Verdict: DROPPED.** The opening is real but crowded.

5. **Restaurant POS / back office**
   - Pain: Toast's fee backlash, 3-year contracts, mandatory payments.
   - Devil's advocate: hardware, payments licensing and 24/7 support make this capital-intensive. Toast, Square, Clover and SpotOn dominate, and Owner.com took the online-ordering unbundle.
   - **Verdict: DROPPED** for a solo or small team.

6. **Construction PM for small GCs and specialty subcontractors**
   - Price paid: Procore about $15K–$30K/yr for small GCs, renewals +10–14%.
   - Pain: "wasn't designed for companies their size."
   - Devil's advocate: Buildertrend, Contractor Foreman ($49/mo), Fieldwire (free tier), Projul and OneCrew already target small contractors. Only sub-trade niches (e.g., paving/concrete, as OneCrew does) look open.
   - **Verdict: SURVIVES (narrow, low confidence)**, only for a specific trade's workflow (T&M tickets, daily logs, change orders, crew mobile), not general construction PM.

7. **Insurance agency management (small independent P&C agencies, about 1–20 staff)**
   - Signals: 70–78% of Epic/AMS360 G2 reviewers come from companies with 50 or fewer employees; constant "too many clicks / steep learning curve" complaints; the TAM sunset pushes legacy users to migrate (157 agencies in one year).
   - Price paid: not found (gap).
   - Switching cost: high. Decades of policy and accounting data, plus carrier download integrations (my inference; not sourced).
   - Devil's advocate: Applied offers EZLynx for small agencies, and HawkSoft, NowCerts and Agency Matrix exist. The domain is regulated, accounting-heavy and sold through trust networks. A US-only market is hard to sell into from Israel.
   - **Verdict: SURVIVES (conditional).** The best-documented "SMBs on enterprise-style tools plus forced migration" combination found. Needs validation of the existing small-agency challengers, which were not researched here.

8. **Therapy / mental-health private practice EHR**
   - Price paid: SimplePractice $49/mo Starter after the +69% hike (March 2025); 200K+ practitioners.
   - Devil's advocate: TherapyNotes, Jane, Headway/Alma (free for in-network clinicians) and many AI-notes tools. HIPAA and insurance-billing compliance is heavy. My knowledge, not sourced this session: Headway and Alma are well-funded.
   - **Verdict: DROPPED** in the US. A self-pay or non-US therapist niche might survive but wasn't researched.

9. **Dental practice management**
   - Pain: Dentrix clicks, slowness and outages.
   - Devil's advocate: very high switching costs (imaging, insurance claims, charting history); Open Dental is already the cheap, beloved alternative; cloud challengers exist (Curve, tab32 — not verified this session).
   - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

10. **Veterinary practice management**
    - Pain: Cornerstone too pricey for small clinics; Avimark outdated; Impromed stagnant.
    - Devil's advocate: modern cloud challengers already exist (ezyVet, Provet, Digitail, Shepherd, Vetspire — named by [Provet](https://www.provet.com/blog/top-10-veterinary-software-solutions-2025); funding not verified). Corporate consolidation of clinics shrinks the independent segment (inference).
    - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

11. **Home care agency software (non-medical)**
    - Pain: WellSky's buggy caregiver app, EVV/GPS issues, mileage errors.
    - Devil's advocate: AxisCare is well liked (4.7/5, 236 G2 reviews), and AlayaCare and Axxess compete. State-by-state EVV and Medicaid compliance is heavy for a small team.
    - **Verdict: DROPPED** as a full suite. A narrow caregiver mobile, recruiting or scheduling add-on is a possible wedge but unvalidated.

12. **Small trucking fleets (1–10 trucks) TMS**
    - Signals: only 17% of fleets under 5 trucks use a TMS (big white space). But a single-truck owner calls $20/mo "expensive", so willingness to pay is very low.
    - Devil's advocate: many cheap tools exist (TruckingOffice, Truckpedia, Datatruck, Tailwind from $99/mo, Bobtail), and carrier churn is high in down freight cycles (inference).
    - **Verdict: DROPPED** (low willingness to pay).

13. **Small freight brokerages (TMS)**
    - Price paid: Aljex Essential from $499/mo, "expensive for smaller brokers"; McLeod's add-on costs and long onboarding.
    - Devil's advocate: Aljex is still rated 4.6/5 (432 reviews), and Revenova, Tai, Rose Rocket and Alvys exist. Brokerage counts swing with freight cycles.
    - **Verdict: SURVIVES (low confidence).** Thin evidence; needs customer interviews.

14. **Nonprofit donor CRM (small nonprofits)**
    - Pain: Blackbaud's new fees with 43 days' notice; value score 3.7/5.
    - Devil's advocate: Bloomerang, Neon CRM, Little Green Light, DonorPerfect and free Givebutter-style tools already serve small nonprofits (Bloomerang and Neon are on [Capterra compare](https://www.capterra.com/compare/131207-247647/Bloomerang-vs-Blackbaud-Raiser-s-Edge-NXT); the others are my knowledge). Budgets are tight.
    - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

15. **Church management**
    - Pain: Planning Center's first hike in 18 years; Tithely's mid-year increase; Pushpay's auto-renewals.
    - Devil's advocate: Planning Center is still beloved and lets customers change plans freely. The market is crowded, and US-only cultural fit makes it hard from Israel.
    - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

16. **Childcare / after-school admin**
    - Pain: Procare's "extreme monthly price increase" and add-ons.
    - Devil's advocate: brightwheel and many others are well funded (not verified this session), and Jackrabbit shows little pain.
    - **Verdict: DROPPED.**

17. **Event venue management**
    - Tripleseat is rated 4.7/5; the only complaints are price and post-acquisition hikes.
    - **Verdict: DROPPED** (weak pain signal).

18. **EHS/safety compliance for companies under 50 employees**
    - Pain: enterprise EHS is opaque, quote-only and "VERY expensive... overkill" for an 8-person firm; renewal hikes push customers out (VelocityEHS).
    - Devil's advocate: SafetyCulture and other mobile inspection apps already cover much of the SMB space (my knowledge; not verified this session). Compliance rules are country-specific, which is also a localization moat.
    - **Verdict: SURVIVES (conditional)**, by sub-vertical and jurisdiction (e.g., Israeli occupational-safety requirements; not researched).

19. **Israeli-localized vertical ops software with ITA allocation-number invoicing**
    - Forcing function: the NIS 5,000 threshold since June 1, 2026 makes allocation numbers mandatory on most domestic B2B invoices, and US vertical SaaS doesn't support this (inference).
    - Devil's advocate: Israel is a small market, and local invoicing tools (Morning/Green Invoice, iCount, Rivhit, Priority) may already add vertical features or integrations (not verified). The mandate doesn't cover B2C verticals (salons, restaurants).
    - **Verdict: SURVIVES (needs validation)**, for B2B service verticals in Israel (contractors, maintenance, B2B services), as a beachhead before going global.

20. **QuickBooks Desktop refugees**
    - The 2024 stop-sell creates migration pain.
    - Devil's advocate: Xero, QuickBooks Online, Zoho and accountant-led migrations already absorb these users.
    - **Verdict: DROPPED** as a standalone product. It is useful as a trigger event for vertical tools that bundle accounting.

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence strength times openness for a small Israeli team: (1) Israeli B2B-vertical ops plus ITA invoicing (unfair local advantage, regulatory forcing function); (2) insurance agency management for small agencies (enterprise-style tools, forced TAM migration, though US-centric and high-switching); (3) SMB EHS by jurisdiction or sub-vertical; (4) small freight brokerage TMS; (5) specialty-subcontractor construction. Everything else is DROPPED, mainly for crowding by funded challengers.
- A cross-cutting wedge that works in any vertical, as a feature rather than a company: transparent monthly pricing, no contract, free data export, and an AI layer that replaces admin labor (the a16z labor-spend thesis). An incumbent that relies on lock-in can't match this quickly. Easy-to-copy pricing alone isn't a moat.

### Gaps
- TAM numbers per category (count of US independent insurance agencies, small freight brokerages, SMB EHS buyers, Israeli B2B service SMBs) were not retrieved in this session.
- The challenger landscape for insurance AMS (HawkSoft, NowCerts, EZLynx), freight brokerage and Israeli vertical tools was not researched in depth. Verdicts marked "conditional" or "low confidence" depend on it.
- No app-store or Reddit primary data. All review quotes need re-verification on the live pages.
